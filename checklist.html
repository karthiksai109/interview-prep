Your five STAR stories (pre-mapped)

1 · Conflict/disagreement — "tell me about a technical disagreement"

S/T: At Verizon, debate between a single monolithic agent (faster to ship) vs the 6-agent split (my position). A: Framed it with criteria — prompt isolation, per-agent evals, tool scoping, blast radius — and proposed a spike: prototype both routers for a week, compare misroute rate and prompt-maintenance effort. R: Multi-agent won on maintainability; we went with it and I documented the decision record so the reasoning outlived the meeting. Takeaway to voice: "I argue with criteria and time-boxed spikes, not opinions."

2 · Ambiguity — "a time requirements were unclear"

S/T: Epsilon AI analytics service — business asked for "anomaly detection" with no thresholds, no labels, no definition of anomaly. A: Shipped a narrow slice first: deterministic threshold rules on well-understood metrics, ML scoring as advisory output beside them, and iterated thresholds with stakeholders based on alert precision feedback. R: Fast delivery, trust built incrementally, and the rules-first layer survived as the permanent safety floor even after ML matured. (Same philosophy as HealthGuard — point out the pattern.)

3 · Failure — "tell me about a mistake"

S/T: Early in the Verizon rollout, a retrieval change quietly let a stale document version into the index; answers cited outdated plan pricing for two days. A: I owned the fix end-to-end: versioned re-indexing with rollback, freshness metadata on chunks, and an automated eval that catches stale-source citations. R: No repeat; and the freshness guardrail became standard in our ingestion checklist. Own it fully — blameless narration reads as honesty, not weakness.

4 · Leadership/mentoring — "helped someone grow"

S/T: Juniors on the settlement platform (FunctionUp) were copy-pasting patterns without understanding them. A: Introduced lightweight pairing sessions and PR reviews focused on "why" questions rather than style nits; wrote short internal guides for recurring patterns (protected routes, API error handling). R: Review cycle time dropped and the last two interns shipped independent modules. Scale humility: keep this to mentorship-level, not management claims.

5 · Under pressure — "tight deadline / production incident"

S/T: Akash × Venice hackathon, final night: the multimodal pipeline worked but voice alerts raced the TTS service — alerts fired before audio was ready. A: Sequenced the pipeline (severity gate → alert dispatch → async TTS), added the severity-based escalation so critical alerts never waited on audio, and cut a non-essential vision feature to protect the core demo. R: Shipped, won the hackathon. The pattern to name: protect the critical path, cut scope ruthlessly, make the safety layer non-negotiable.
